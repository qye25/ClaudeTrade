# Portfolio Policy

## 1. Goal

The policy exists to keep portfolio changes inside deterministic risk limits while also allowing tax-aware trade review. The goal is not to maximize tax benefit at the cost of violating risk rules.

## 2. Hard priorities

1. Capital preservation and loss prevention
2. Hard risk limits
3. Leverage and concentration constraints
4. Tax-aware selection within valid choices
5. Signal quality and expected return
6. Transaction efficiency

The engine should never propose a portfolio change solely because the model is bullish or bearish if that change breaches hard constraints.

## 3. Leverage-aware exposure

The initial effective leverage approximation is:

- NFLX: 1x
- RKLB: 1x
- TSLA: 1x
- UPRO: 3x
- DFEN: 3x

Effective leverage approximation:

```
portfolio_effective_leverage =
    NFLX_weight
  + RKLB_weight
  + TSLA_weight
  + 3 * UPRO_weight
  + 3 * DFEN_weight
```

This is a deterministic approximation used for policy checks, not a full risk model.

## 4. Concentration constraints

The engine should calculate the following for each holding:

- position_value
- position_weight
- effective_leverage_contribution
- concentration bucket

The policy must allow per-asset and per-theme concentration limits. This includes:

- individual stock concentration
- leveraged ETF concentration
- sector or theme concentration
- total leveraged exposure

A holding may be unacceptable even when its dollar value is small if its effective exposure is large.

## 5. Rules for rebalancing

Rebalancing is a constrained optimization problem. The system should search for the least-cost portfolio change that satisfies hard constraints.

Preferred selection order:

1. Risk reduction
2. Leverage reduction
3. Concentration reduction
4. Tax cost minimization
5. Expected return improvement
6. Transaction efficiency

The system may not choose a higher-tax solution when a lower-tax solution keeps the portfolio inside the policy limits.

## 6. Tax-aware lot selection

Lot-level data is authoritative when available.

For each tax lot the system should model:

- symbol
- quantity
- cost basis
- acquisition date
- term classification (short/long)
- current price / value
- unrealized gain or loss
- realized gain or loss if sold
- wash-sale relevance if available

Tax calculations are for deterministic estimation only. They are not tax advice.

## 7. Tax profile

The default profile should be configurable and explicit:

- filing_status: single
- estimated_taxable_income: 180000
- state: WA
- account_type: taxable_brokerage

Estimated tax components should be separated as:

- federal_tax_estimate
- washington_tax_estimate
- niit_estimate

Every result must carry the label:

> Estimated tax impact — not tax advice.

## 8. Wash sale status

The engine should eventually check:

- recent purchases
- planned replacement purchases
- same or similar security exposure
- 61-day wash-sale window
- loss-harvest compatibility

Until that information exists, the safer state is:

- wash_sale_status = UNKNOWN

The engine should not assume a loss harvest is valid without the required supporting data.

## 9. TradingAgents and Python policy separation

### Analysis layer

TradingAgents/Gemini may analyze:

- which names look attractive or weak
- which assets may be overweight or underweight
- expected direction and risk factors

### Policy layer

Python decides:

- whether a change is allowed
- which lots may be sold
- whether the trade keeps the portfolio inside leverage and concentration limits
- the tax impact of the candidate trade
- whether the final proposed portfolio is compliant

This prevents the LLM from overriding deterministic portfolio controls.

## 10. Robinhood safety rules

The current Robinhood adapter is read-only and uses an allowlist:

- get_accounts
- get_portfolio
- get_equity_positions
- get_equity_quotes
- get_equity_tax_lots
- get_equity_tradability

Everything else is blocked, including live trading tools.

## 11. Live execution status

Live execution is disabled. The engine must remain in read/review mode until explicit approval and execution gating have been implemented.

## 12. Final review logic

Every candidate trade must pass a final review:

1. Is the candidate portfolio inside hard risk limits?
2. Does the trade reduce leverage or concentration as required?
3. Which actual lots would be sold?
4. What is the estimated tax impact?
5. Does the final portfolio remain compliant after the trade is sized and modified?
6. Is the proposal still valid after the final risk + tax check?

A trade is not valid merely because it was generated by the model or initially looked tax efficient.
